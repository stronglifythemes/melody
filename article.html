How to prepare your songs before a mixing session

A few simple steps before you send your files will save hours in the studio and keep the mix focused on what matters most: the music.

A great mix starts long before the first fader moves. When the session files arrive organized and complete, the engineer can spend their time on balance, depth and emotion instead of searching for missing tracks.

These are the steps we recommend to every artist before a mixing session at Melody. None of them require special equipment, only a little time and attention.

Organize and label your tracks

Clear names make a session easy to read at a glance. Use short, descriptive labels and group related instruments together so nothing gets lost.

  • Name every file by instrument and part, for example "Vocal Lead Verse".
  • Export all tracks from the same starting point so they line up.
  • Remove muted takes and experiments you do not want in the mix.

Leave room for the mix

Avoid heavy compression or limiting on the master bus of your rough mix. Export the tracks without processing unless an effect is part of the sound you want to keep.

"The best mixes happen when the artist arrives with a clear idea of how the song should feel."

Share a reference

Bring one or two songs whose sound you admire. A reference is not something to copy, but it helps everyone in the room speak the same language.

The console where most of our mixes take shape.

Write down your notes

  1. List the moments in the song that matter most to you.
  2. Note any sounds or effects that must stay as they are.
  3. Mention anything from the rough mix you want to change.
